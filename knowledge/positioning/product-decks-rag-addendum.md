# The Monster - deck-derived sales knowledge

## Core product story

The Monster is positioned as the world's biggest inflatable party and the world's largest inflatable obstacle-course experience. The promoter deck frames it as a high-energy, festival-style attraction built around adult nostalgia, group fun, music, spectacle, and social sharing.

The key emotional hook is: adults get to relive the feeling of 1990s game shows, obstacle-course TV, and childhood inflatable fun in a large-scale event format.

Useful sales-language ideas:

- World's biggest inflatable party.
- Ultimate party playground.
- 300 metres of bouncy mayhem.
- A giant 1990s game-show fantasy brought to life.
- A festival-style inflatable experience with music, food, bars, lighting, visuals, and social-photo moments.
- A headline attraction that can be adapted for adults, families, kids, corporate groups, and competitive sessions.

## Experience description

The deck describes a full Monster event as more than the inflatable course alone. The wider experience can include:

- A 300-metre inflatable obstacle course.
- 42 obstacles.
- Live DJs.
- Giant flamingo and unicorn ball pits.
- Giant inflatable set dressing.
- Street food.
- Bars.
- Live action cameras on a big screen.
- High-end sound, lighting, and special effects.
- Start and finish moments designed for photos and group celebration.

For copilot responses, describe this as an event concept that can be scaled up or down depending on what the promoter is responsible for providing. The checklist makes clear that production elements such as bar, food, lighting, sound, staging, and extra event production are not automatically included unless agreed.

## Track record and proof points

The Monster deck gives these proof points:

- First launched in London in 2017.
- First major event was at Alexandra Palace.
- Sold 20,000 tickets within four weeks for the first event.
- Has toured 35 cities.
- Has operated in 9 countries.
- Has sold more than 450,000 tickets.
- Has operated across the UK, Europe, the Middle East, the USA, and Canada.
- Example deck locations include Miami Beach 2023, Abu Dhabi New Year's Day 2021, and Victoria, Canada 2023.

Use these as credibility points when a lead asks whether the attraction is proven, scalable, or internationally tourable.

## Venue and format positioning

The Monster deck presents the product as versatile across:

- Sports arenas.
- Indoor arenas.
- Conference centres.
- Parks.
- Beaches.
- Resort-style outdoor spaces.

The visual site-plan pages show the Monster as a flexible outdoor festival layout with a main arena, stage zone, entrance/exit area, bars, food trucks, queueing, seating, furniture, fencing, and branded dressing. These are useful for helping promoters imagine the event, but they should not be treated as an included production package.

## Event format options

The deck says the Monster does not always need to be an adult party format. It can also be positioned for:

- Team-building.
- Sport and fun-fitness concepts.
- Parent-and-kids events.
- Dedicated kids-only events.
- Family fun days.
- Competition formats.
- Corporate events.
- Sports clubs and active social groups.

This is useful for lead matching. The Monster is strongest when a promoter wants a ticketable, scheduled, high-energy experience with clear group appeal.

## Audience profile and appeal

The deck gives these audience claims:

- 68% female.
- 75% aged 20-35.
- 25% aged 36-44.
- Audience described as experience seekers.
- Broad appeal includes pleasure seekers, fitness fans, groups, hen parties, bachelor parties, birthday parties, corporate groups, team-building groups, families, competitors, and sports clubs.
- The event can also work for kids and families where the format is appropriate.

Use this for sales positioning and audience fit. Do not use it as a guarantee of local attendance demographics.

## Press and fan language

The deck includes press and fan quotes. Use these carefully as marketing flavour rather than operational facts.

Press-style language shown in the deck includes:

- Time Out: relive every 90s game-show fantasy.
- Evening Standard: tremendous fun.
- The Independent: every childhood game show crammed into one long bouncy dream.
- London in Stereo: if you get no joy from the Monster, you're an animal.

Fan-comment themes include:

- Non-stop laughs.
- Amazing tunes.
- Crazy fun and totally different.
- Crying with laughter.
- Can we go again?
- Absolutely incredible.

The copilot can use these themes to help draft promotional copy, but should avoid overusing exact quotes unless the user specifically asks for deck-style marketing language.

## Visual context from the deck

The deck visuals emphasize:

- Big, colourful inflatable structures.
- A giant monster-mouth entrance.
- Large slides and obstacle sections.
- Bright tropical/set-dressing elements.
- Party lighting and festival energy.
- Start/finish signage.
- Social-media-friendly photo moments.
- Outdoor festival layouts with food, drink, stage, seating, and queue control.

This context helps the copilot describe the product in richer natural language when responding to leads.

# Mega Bounce House - deck-derived sales knowledge

## Core product story

The Mega Bounce House is positioned as the world's biggest bounce house and a large-scale standalone destination attraction. The deck frames it as colourful, family-friendly, visually massive, and easy for guests to understand immediately.

Useful sales-language ideas:

- The world's biggest bounce house.
- 27,000 square feet of bouncy madness.
- A destination attraction, not just a side activity.
- A visual landmark that draws attention from a distance.
- A high-energy, high-capacity inflatable experience for kids, adults, and families.
- Ideal for creating social-media moments and drawing large crowds.

## Experience description

The deck describes Mega Bounce House as a vast inflatable environment with:

- Oversized characters.
- Large architectural inflatable elements.
- Sports zones.
- Obstacle-course style areas.
- Slides.
- Open bounce areas.
- Colourful side dressing.
- Tall walls and oversized visual features.

The experience is free-flowing rather than race-based. It is easy for mixed-age audiences to understand and is especially strong for daytime, family, and high-footfall public events.

## Audience and event fit

Mega Bounce House is best positioned for:

- Family-focused festivals.
- City and council events.
- Public-space activations.
- High-footfall destination attractions.
- Daytime programming.
- Events where the promoter wants visual impact with less need for a competitive or timed race concept.

The deck shows both children and adults using the attraction, so the copilot should not describe it as kids-only. It is family-friendly and intergenerational.

## Deck proof and scale claims

The deck gives these sales facts:

- 27,000 square feet.
- Walls over 26 feet high.
- Capacity of 250 people at one time.
- Recommended minimum experience duration of 30 minutes.

Use these for sales positioning, but do not use deck operational specs to override checklist qualification requirements.

## Visual context from the deck

The Mega Bounce House deck visuals emphasize:

- Large purple, teal, pink, orange, and yellow inflatable structures.
- A giant central character/mascot feature.
- Slides, climbing points, balance elements, sports-style play zones, and obstacle features.
- Inflatable palm-tree dressing and large playful architectural forms.
- A bright family-friendly visual identity.
- Strong aerial and photo-friendly impact.

This context helps the copilot describe Mega Bounce House as colourful, playful, and highly visible.

# Product matching guidance derived from the decks

## Choose The Monster when the lead wants

- A headline, ticketed experience.
- A scheduled or session-based event.
- Adult nostalgia or 1990s game-show energy.
- Corporate team-building or competitive formats.
- A festival atmosphere with DJs, music, food, drink, lighting, and social content.
- A proven international attraction with a strong ticket-sales history.
- A product that can be marketed to adults, groups, celebrations, corporate teams, and active audiences.

## Choose Mega Bounce House when the lead wants

- A simpler destination attraction.
- A large visual draw for a mixed-age or family audience.
- A free-flowing bounce environment rather than a competitive obstacle-course format.
- Daytime public programming.
- A family/city/council/festival attraction.
- High simultaneous capacity and strong visual impact.

## Escalate rather than auto-match when

- The venue dimensions are borderline.
- The lead is asking for exact site-plan feasibility.
- The lead needs territory exclusivity.
- The lead is negotiating price, deposit, or cancellation terms.
- The lead asks whether production elements such as stage, bar, food, sound, lighting, or full event build are included.
- The lead is a school, charity, kids' birthday party, or low-budget community event.
- The lead asks for local safety certification, permits, insurance wording, anchoring approval, or weather-operation rules.

The Monster is a large-scale inflatable obstacle-course and party experience. Answer as a destination attraction first, then qualify operational details from the checklist.

Recommended answer style:

Mega Bounce House is a huge, colourful touring bounce-house attraction designed as a standalone destination for families, kids, adults, and high-footfall public events. It is promoted as 27,000 square feet, with oversized characters, slides, sports zones, obstacle-style areas, and open bounce spaces. # Known conflicts and duplicated information to keep out of the main RAG answer set

The following deck or old-KB details overlap with, or conflict with, `full-checklist.md`. The copilot should not treat them as primary qualification facts.

## Mega Bounce House conflicts

- The Mega Bounce House deck says minimum space is 50m x 50m. The checklist says minimum floor space is 75m x 60m and ceiling height minimum is 9m. Use the checklist.
- The deck says minimum 6 event staff. The checklist says event staffing is typically 7-13 plus setup/packdown crew, provided by the promoter. Use the checklist.
- The deck says setup is 8 hours, packdown is 6 hours, with 14 crew. The checklist gives the current staffing reality and promoter responsibilities. Use the checklist for qualification and delivery planning.
- The old KB repeats the deck's 50m x 50m space and 6-staff figures. Treat these as deprecated.

## Monster conflicts

- The Monster deck says ideal space is 90m x 50m with 7m height clearance, and 70m x 35m for the inflatable only. The checklist says Monster requires minimum 35m x 90m and minimum 6m ceiling clearance. Use the checklist for qualification.
- The deck says The Monster supplies 1 technician and 1 tour/staffing manager. The checklist says one Event Manager is provided. Use the checklist unless Nick confirms otherwise for a particular deal.
- The deck production rider lists many event elements such as stage, sound, lighting, LED screen, bars, food trucks, cloakroom, barriers, fencing, paramedics, security, radios, and optional smoke/haze. The checklist says these are promoter responsibilities unless full event production is agreed. Do not present them as included.
- The old KB repeats several deck technical specs and should not be used as the operational source of truth.

## Commercial and legal duplication
